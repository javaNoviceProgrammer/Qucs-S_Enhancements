# RF 2.5D, phase D: de-embedding, and each port's own reference

The proposal's phase D is "ports and de-embedding: edge and internal ports, calibration lines,
renormalisation, the S-matrix and Touchstone, passivity and reciprocity checks". `1db0e32` brought most of
it: the ports, each port into its own reference, the S-matrix, Touchstone 1.1, and the two checks. Two
pieces were left:
- an edge port's own discontinuity, which every solve still carried in its answer;
- the "De-embed" length the edge port has always had, which the solve only noted as not done.

The user said "rebuild the dmg and then start phase D" (1 November); the DMG was rebuilt at `2e31d40` first.
This round adds:
- de-embedding of an edge port by calibration strips of its feed;
- a reference plane moved along that feed, or along a line the user gives;
- Touchstone 2.0, which carries each port's own reference.

## What it does now

- **An edge port's own discontinuity.** An edge port drives the face of its conductor as a gap. What a
  circuit connects there is the line the face ends, but the port adds a discontinuity of its own: the
  face's fringing charge, the current's turn into the face, and a reference half a cell inside the face.
  That is the port's, not the structure's, and it can now be taken out.
- **By two strips of the port's feed.** The strips are as wide as the face, on the port's metal, ℓ and 2ℓ
  long, meshed as the structure was, each with a port of the same kind at both ends. ℓ is eight times the
  metal's height over the ground and twice the width (8h + 2w), so that the two ports' near fields do not
  meet. Then:
  - `T_ℓ T_2ℓ⁻¹ T_ℓ = E Ē`, where T is a strip's chain matrix as solved and E the port's discontinuity from
    the port to its face. This needs nothing of the line at all.
  - E Ē fixes E only up to an ideal transformer at the face, which nothing measured on lines can fix.
    Taking the discontinuity as a series element at the port and a shunt element at the face (E's D one)
    fixes it, and keeps the port's own voltage and current at the face.
  - The line between is then `L(ℓ) = E⁻¹ T_ℓ Ē⁻¹`: its propagation constant and its impedance in that
    voltage and current.
  - The strips' phase is followed up the sweep from a frequency below it, where the shorter strip is a few
    degrees long. A strip may be several half waves long at the top of a sweep, and only that anchor says
    how many turns its phase has made.
- **Taken out.** At each port, `[V; I] = F [V'; I']`, F the port's E and the line of its shift. Then
  `Z' = (A − Z C)⁻¹ (Z D − B)`, with F's terms on the diagonals, and S follows from Z' with each port into
  its own reference.
- **The port's own settings:**
  - "Take out its own discontinuity" (off by default);
  - "De-embed", how far the reference plane is then moved in along the line (out, below nought);
  - "Moved along": its feed, as the strips say (any shift then takes the discontinuity out too), or a line
    of given impedance and effective permittivity, the proposal's "or by a given Z0 and γ", which moves
    the plane with nothing else taken out and no strips solved.
- **In the tab.** After the structure's solve, one calibration is solved for each feed (its metal and its
  width), and the ports are taken back. The status line says "calibrating the ports" meanwhile. Stop
  stops the strips too, and closing the tab does.
- **What each port had** is said in four places, each at the top of the sweep: the Results node, the
  solve's lines, the Touchstone file's comments, and `rf25_solve`'s answer. That is:
  - where its reference plane is;
  - its feed's line: impedance, effective permittivity, loss;
  - the discontinuity, as a shunt C at the face and a series L at the port.
- **The checks after de-embedding.** Reciprocity and passivity are of the de-embedded answer, and the
  solve's own are kept beside them. A de-embedding can leave a little of what the strips and the
  structure's own feed differ by, which is the de-embedding's, not the solve's, and the Results say which
  it is.
- **The currents** are drawn as the ports drive them in the solve, whatever their reference planes were
  moved to.
- **Touchstone 2.0** writes each port into its own reference: `[Reference]`, the two-port's
  `[Two-Port Data Order] 21_12`, `[Network Data]` and `[End]`. 1.1 stays the default, because a
  schematic's S-parameter file part hands its file to ngspice through `s2spice`, which reads Touchstone 1
  only. The Touchstone node's "Version" chooses, and `rf25_export` takes `version`. A quasi-static
  solve's lines are written as 2.0 too when it is asked, every port into the export's own reference.
  `rf25_export` says which version it wrote, and for a full-wave 2.0 file each port's reference.

## What building it found

- **Strips of four heights were too short.** The ports' near fields met over them, and what was taken for
  the port's own discontinuity was the two ports' together:
  - at four heights, a line de-embedded to a thru was 0.4° out at 20 GHz, |S11| 4e-3, and eps_eff 2 % low;
  - at eight heights, 0.005° and 5e-4.

  Hence 8h + 2w.
- **Two strips can read radiation as the line's.** A little of what the two ports radiate to each other
  comes out as the line's loss or gain. On 100 µm of 9.8 that is a few parts in 1e3 of a strip, inside the
  de-embedding's own error. On 254 µm of alumina, from 20 GHz, the substrate's surface wave carries it
  between the ports, and it falls off too slowly for any length of strip to put them apart: the line came
  out giving back 1 to 3 % over a strip. The calibration now says so where a line gives back more than
  half a percent. A multiline calibration is what cures it, and is not done.
- **The normalisation works.** The feed's impedance in the port's own voltage and current comes out within
  0.3 % of phase B's quasi-static line, which rests on no port at all.
- **Two things seen, not changed here:**
  - The full-wave line's eps_eff runs about 1 % low at four cells across a strip (0.992 for vacuum, where it
    is 1), and converges as the mesh is refined.
  - De-embedding does not cure the small non-passivity of a port in a stack all of vacuum (2e-5 of a wave,
    found in `2e31d40`). It is not confined to the port: the port's missing vertical current radiates to
    the whole line.
- **The schematic's S-parameter part reads only Touchstone 1** (`s2spice`), so 2.0 is an option, not the
  default.
- **Slips in the tests themselves.** A step's S22 was expected smaller into 25 Ohm than into 50, but port 2
  looks through the step into port 1's 50 Ohm. A test's `says()` matched "µm" through `QLatin1String`,
  which never matches non-ASCII. And a test expected the strips' loss to rise with frequency, where a
  lossless bound mode has none.

## What the breaks taught

- **Eighteen of the twenty were caught at once.** Two were not:
  - **E's A one, not its D, was no fault.** The two normalisations differ by an ideal transformer of
    √(A/D) at the face. E is reciprocal, so with D one, A is 1 + BC: the series element at the port times
    the shunt one at the face. For the thru's port that is 1.4e-3 at 20 GHz (4.6 fF and −19.6 pH), a
    transformer of 1.0007. That is the ambiguity nothing measured on lines can fix, and no test could tell
    the two apart. The break became a transformer of √2 at the face, which the feed's impedance against
    phase B's catches.
  - **Every edge port calibrated unasked was a gap.** The tab read the port's "calibrate" straight from the
    node's properties, and a node never edited has none there, so the model's default was never read. Had
    the default been on, the editor would have shown it on and the solve would have left it off. The tab
    now reads it as it reads every other flag, the kind's default where the node has none, and the tab's
    test catches the break.
- After the fix the two were run again, with the two whose lines it moved: all caught, twenty of twenty.

The full ctest (149), the scenario script (97 checks) and both lints were green. Under the sanitizers the
new suite takes 169 s, no function more than 48; the tab's full-wave test 19 s, up from 13, and the tools'
10, up from 6. CI's sanitized job gives a suite three times its 300 s. The tab's suite takes 281 s, 221 of
them its solve straight after an edit, whose CI failure at `3ae9608` is still set aside.

## What the DMG's check found

Before the DMG of `45dc14b` was kept, the packaged app's MCP server was driven as the DMG's checks ask. It
solved a calibrated port and wrote Touchstone 2.0 as it should, but its answer of that file was wrong:
- `rf25_export` called the 2.0 file "Touchstone 1.1", and said a schematic's S-parameter part reads it at
  once, which it does not;
- it gave every port the one `impedance` asked, where the file gave each port its own;
- and 2.0 asked of a quasi-static solve was written as 1.1, with no word said.

The answer now names the version it wrote, gives a full-wave 2.0 file's references port by port, and says
a schematic's part reads 1.1 alone. A quasi-static solve's lines are written as 2.0 when it is asked, every
port into the export's reference, the data as 1.1 writes them. Six breaks more hold it, and all six were
caught at once: twenty-six of twenty-six. Under the sanitizers the three tests it touched take 9, 11 and
44 s, the last almost all its quasi-static solves.

  on a polygon whose feed tapers is calibrated as a straight strip of its face, and meshed as one.
