# RF 2.5D's bandstop filter against Palace 3D

Phase 7 of the plan for RF 2.5D's accuracy limits. It answers A4 of the open items of 8 November: a bandstop filter
read 1.4–2.1 % off in frequency, on a design that is not on this machine. The plan replaced it with our own filter,
solved by RF 2.5D and Palace 3D.

**The answer:** RF 2.5D's stopband is Palace's within 0.1 % at 3 cells across, and within 0.32 % at 12. The plan
asked for the notch within 0.5 % and the width within 2 %. No solver code changed.

## The filter

`filter.py`, with dimensions chosen here.
- **The board:** a 50 Ω microstrip 250 µm wide on 254 µm of alumina (εr 9.8) over a ground plane, lossless.
- **The stubs:** three pairs of open stubs, one either side of the line, each 250 µm wide and 2850 µm from the line's
  edge. That is a quarter wave at 10 GHz less a closed-form open end of 80 µm. The pairs are 2925 µm apart, with
  1.5 mm of feed at each end, 8.85 mm in all.
- **Why pairs:** the filter is then symmetric about its axis, so Palace can solve half of it.
- **What is compared:** the stopband's −20 dB edges, their centre and their width. The least |S21| is not a usable
  feature here: it lies in a flat floor, −69 dB in RF 2.5D from 10.4 to 11.2 GHz and −82 dB in Palace.

## The runs

- **RF 2.5D** (`make_rf25.py`): edge ports at the feeds' ends, de-embedded, 4–16 GHz.
  - 6 and 12 cells across agree within 0.1 %.
  - Calibrated and uncalibrated ports agree within 0.01 %.
- **Palace** (`make_palace.py`): the same board in 3D, as a half model with a magnetic wall on the axis.
  - The box: the board as large as the box, with a first-order absorbing boundary on its far side and top.
  - The ports: wave ports on the board's two ends.
  - The solve: order 2 with the adaptive driven sweep.
  - Each run was alone on the machine, under a guard that would have stopped it if available memory fell under
    6 GB. The filter peaked at 22.8 GB, and no run came near the limit.
- **Two set-up faults in my own scripts**, found and fixed before these runs:
  - **The metal was misidentified.** Metal faces were picked as those whose centre lies inside the filter's outline.
    The rest of the alumina's top is comb-shaped, and its centre falls in a stub, so the whole top was taken for
    metal. The board then became a parallel plate, which wave ports could not launch a microstrip mode into. The
    metal is now what the outline itself became in the mesh.
  - **Lumped ports on the end faces leaked.** They lost most of the power into the absorbing boundary beside them: a
    plain 50 Ω line gave S21 −4.5 dB and S11 −11 to −19 dB. With wave ports that line gives S11 −56 dB and S21
    −0.002 dB.
  - The pads' meshes of phase 6 were checked for the first fault: their metal areas are the pads' exactly
    (0.1423 and 0.2348 mm²). The pads script now takes its perfect sheets the same way.

## Results

| | Palace | RF 2.5D, 3 across (the test) | RF 2.5D, 12 across |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower −20 dB edge | 7.4377 GHz | 7.4323 (−0.07 %) | 7.4507 (+0.17 %) |
| Upper −20 dB edge | 13.6737 GHz | 13.6742 (+0.00 %) | 13.7180 (+0.32 %) |
| Centre | 10.5557 GHz | −0.02 % | +0.27 % |
| Width | 6.2360 GHz | +0.09 % | +0.50 % |

The 3-across figures come from the test, with largest cells of 400 µm. The 12-across ones come from the model in the
app, with its own default largest cell.

**The pieces**, each solved alone:
- **The line:** Palace 2D's εeff is 6.610, 6.664 and 6.750 at 4, 10 and 16 GHz. RF 2.5D's calibration reads 6.736
  at 16 GHz, −0.2 %.
- **One open stub as a one-port in RF 2.5D:** a quarter wave at 9.91 GHz, where its closed form puts it.
- **One pair of stubs alone:**

  | | Palace | RF 2.5D, 12 across | Difference |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Null | 10.9247 GHz | 10.9421 GHz | +0.16 % |
  | −20 dB edges | 10.2541–11.5740 GHz | 10.2644–11.6089 GHz | +0.10 %, +0.30 % |

  Palace's own mesh is settled. Halving its edge size, from 20 to 10 µm, moves the pair by 0.08 %: the null to
  10.9332 GHz and the edges to 10.2624–11.5837, toward RF 2.5D's.

  An ideal transmission-line model puts the pair near 9.9–10.0 GHz, measuring the stubs from the line's edge. Both
  solvers put it 9 % higher: the junction makes the stubs electrically shorter. Hammerstad's T-junction model moves
  a stub's reference plane about 190 µm into it here.

## Checked

- **`test_rf25_accuracy`** `aStubFiltersStopbandIsPalaces` (new):
  - the filter at 3 across, solved at 0.05 GHz steps around each edge;
  - each edge and the centre within 0.5 % of Palace's numbers, stored in the test, and the width within 2 %;
  - it takes 1 s, and passes under ASan and UBSan in 20 s; `test_rf25_accuracy` passes under ctest and the four
    lints pass.
- **`breaks.py`** undoes the physics the test stands on. Its results are in `results.txt` beside it.

## Not done

- **The de-embedding gives power back** where the filter reflects nearly everything: 0.06 % at 12 GHz. The solve
  says it is the de-embedding's. The uncalibrated solve gives back 1e-5 at 6.1 GHz.
- **The stopband's floor:** −69 dB in RF 2.5D against −82 dB in Palace, from 10.4 to 11.2 GHz. It sets no frequency,
  and was not traced.
