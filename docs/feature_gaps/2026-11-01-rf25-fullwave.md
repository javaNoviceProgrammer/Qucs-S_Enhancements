# RF 2.5D, phase C: the full-wave solve

The first round of phase C (`3c8a5d6`, after the user's "Straight to phase C" of 1 November) built the
retarded Green's functions of the stack. This round is what the proposal names next: "the dense fill and
solve, the currents". `rf25/rf25_fullwave.{h,cpp}` solves a mesh at a list of frequencies, with ports, and
returns each frequency's Z and S matrices and the current on every run at the last frequency, driven from
the first port. The tab does not call it yet: wiring it to the model's ports and study is the next
round's (below).

Building it also found an error in phase B: phase B judged its near cells by their area, which left its
C 0.4 % high on a 50 Ohm line and made its answer depend on how the cells were stretched. That is fixed
here too.

## The method

The mixed-potential integral equation, discretized on phase B's mesh as phase B's inductance already
is (PEEC, in Ruehli's terms):

- **The currents** run between cells that share an edge, `runsOf()`: a rooftop on each pair of
  rectangles, piecewise constant from one centre to the other, as wide as the edge. **The charges** are
  on the cells, each spread evenly over its cell, its potential taken at the cell's centre.
- **L**, the runs' partial inductances, is built from the vector kernel. Its singular term and that
  term's image in the ground are phase B's own closed form, the static partial inductance of the two runs
  and of one run with the other's mirror image. Each is scaled by the kernel's coefficient, and the
  image's is −μ0/2. The retardation of both, `(exp(−jkR) − 1)/R`, is smooth and is taken at the runs'
  middles, together with the stack's remainder. Orthogonal runs have no mutual inductance: the
  horizontal kernel has no cross term.
- **P**, the cells' potential coefficients, is built from the scalar kernel:
  - Its singular term is the rectangle's closed form, at whatever height apart the two cells are. Its
    retardation is taken at the centres.
  - The stack's remainder is integrated over the charged cell by Gauss's rule. The number of points is set
    by how the cell compares with the distance the remainder changes over: the distance between the two
    cells, or the remainder's reach (twice the nearest reflecting interface), whichever is longer.
  - The remainder is taken at the cell's centre only where the cell is small beside that distance:
    its longest side squared under 1.2e-3 of the distance squared, which keeps the error under a part
    in 1e4.
  - P is then made symmetric. A potential at a centre of a charge over a cell is not quite the same the
    other way round where two cells differ in size, and the port matrix is reciprocal only if P is
    symmetric.
- **The charge is eliminated**: `(Zs l/w + jw L + Aᵀ P A / jw) I = Aᵀ P J / jw`, a dense complex system
  in the runs' currents, solved by Eigen's partial-pivoting LU at each frequency. A is the incidence of
  runs on cells. The Green's functions are built once per pair of metal heights per frequency, and
  everything static once per solve.
- **A port** drives the face of the conductor it is on, found by phase B's `faceCells()`, which this
  round lifted out of the inductance unchanged:
  - Its current goes into the cells along the face, each cell's share being the length of face it has,
    and comes back through the ground.
  - Its voltage is those cells' potentials averaged by the same shares, which keeps Z symmetric.
  - The reference plane is at those cells' centres, half a cell inside the face.
- **A metal** is a sheet with the surface impedance of a slab whose current runs on both faces:
  `(γ/2σ) coth(γt/2)`, which is `1/(σt)` at direct current and half of `(1 + j)/(σδ)` when thick.
- **S** is `(Z − Z0)(Z + Z0)⁻¹`, with one reference impedance at every port.
- **What it refuses, in so many words**:
  - a via, whose current runs up and down;
  - a stack with no ground plane, since the ports are referred to one;
  - no port, no frequency, a frequency of nought or less, or a reference impedance of none;
  - two ports on one face;
  - more than 3000 runs and cells together. Near the limit, 1874 runs over 984 cells took 0.8 s a
    frequency after 0.4 s once, and some 200 MB.

  A port that is on no edge drives the face nearest it, as the inductance's terminals do, and says which.

## What the tests found, and what it changed

- **Phase B judged its near cells by their area, and was 0.4 % high.** Phase B takes a cell four of the
  pair's sides away as a point charge. The side was the square root of the cell's area, and a line's
  cells are four times as long as they are wide. Judged that way, the cell two lengths along was a point
  charge a part in fifty out.
  - The full-wave's first low-frequency C was 0.47 % below phase B's on a 50 Ohm line.
  - Judged by the longest side, phase B moved 0.39 % onto it. With phase B's bound at twelve sides the two
    met to 3e-5.
  - The stretched mesh now gives what square cells give. Phase B's Z0 on ten cells across is 0.79 % above
    Hammerstad and Jensen, and 0.36 % on twenty, halving with the cells. Its own test comment records
    0.8 % for square cells. Before, it was 0.37 % and 0.13 %: the far cells' point charges had been
    cancelling part of the error by chance.
  - Phase B's suites pass unchanged, and their notes carry the new figures.
- **The solve's own remainder had the same fault, and worse.** Its first rule for when to integrate the
  remainder over a cell copied phase B's: four times the square root of the area. On 25 x 100 µm cells,
  the ground's image 200 µm down was taken at cell centres where it was a per cent out. C came out
  2.4e-3 low. It is now judged against the distance the remainder changes over, as described above,
  and lands within 7.4e-5 of every pair in closed form.
- **A short line's ends do not cancel in the two-line method.**
  - The mutual inductance of two parallel runs a distance d apart has a d²/(4ℓ) term, with d = 2h for a
    run and its image. L per metre came out 419 nH/m from lines of 0.5 and 1 mm on 100 µm, and 422.7
    from 1 and 2 mm, about as that term predicts (4 nH/m).
  - At high frequency the two ports' near fields also reach each other directly. On 635 µm alumina with
    lines of 2 to 6 mm, ε_eff at 10 GHz was twice as dispersed as Kirschning and Jansen, varied with the
    pair of lengths taken, and gave the lossless line an α of 4 to 14 Np/m, the same on meshes of 200 and
    100 µm. A rough estimate of the mutual impedance between two short monopoles of that height gives
    some 15 Ω beside the line's 60.
  - On 100 µm with lines of 20 to 60 heights, the quasi-static ε_eff agreed across every pair of lengths
    to 4e-4, and at 10 GHz with Kirschning and Jansen to within 6e-4.
  - The tests use 2 and 3 mm on 100 µm, and say why at their head. The same effect will be in any
    two-port that is short beside its substrate, and in a real one fed through coaxial pins as much as
    in this model.
- **Symmetrizing P is not free.** It moves C by 1.2e-4 on one metal, and by some 1e-3 on two metals
  whose cells do not line up: most of the difference from phase B in the two-metal case.
- **The test's own two-line Z0** first took each end as a shunt alone, and gave 45 and 47 Ω from the two
  lengths. Each end also has a series part of about −19 pH, from the reference plane at the face cells'
  centres. With both kinds of end the two lengths agree, at 49.59 Ω against √(L′/C′) = 49.60.
- Two slips in the tests themselves: a current read across a line where no run crossed it, since the
  end cells' centres are 12.5 µm in; and a port assumed to drive one cell where phase B's face finder
  snaps it to the nearest face.

## Broken on purpose

`docs/feature_gaps/2026-11-01-rf25-fullwave/breaks.py` holds twenty-two breaks:

- the vector potential's image left out, and taken at the runs themselves;
- the scalar remainder left out, taken at every centre, and integrated by a one-point rule;
- P not made symmetric;
- the charge's incidence with both ends alike;
- jw L with the wrong sign;
- the scalar and the vector retardation each left out, and the vector remainder left out;
- a run's resistance taken across it;
- the sheet's current on one face only;
- S of the wrong inverse;
- a port's shares not summing to one;
- the first pair of heights' Green's function for every pair;
- no current kept;
- a via, no ground, two ports on one face and a frequency of nought each let through;
- phase B's near cells judged by their area again.

## What the breaks taught

- **Twenty were caught at once.** Two came back marked uncaught, and neither was a hole:
  - **P left asymmetric** was caught by the tee. The line's test had been listed against it as well, but
    a line is the same from either end, and so reciprocal whatever P is.
  - **A frequency of nought let through** was still refused, because the retarded Green's functions
    refuse it too, in the same words. They do so only after the static parts are filled and every
    frequency before it solved. The test now holds the solve to its own words, which point to the
    quasi-static extraction for nought, and the break is caught.

  Twenty-two of twenty-two.
- The full ctest (148), the scenario script (97 checks) and both lints were green. Under the
  sanitizers the new suite takes some 56 s, no function more than 13; phase B's static suite, with more
  near pairs now, is 33 s at most, and its S parameters 11.

## Not done

- **Wiring it in.** The model's ports are edge ports, with a facing and a de-embedding, and internal
  ports between two metals or a metal and the ground, each with its own reference impedance. The study
  needs a full-wave solve, the S parameters need to reach the Touchstone writer and the MCP tools, and
  the currents need a picture. This round solves points on faces with one reference impedance.
- **Vias.** A vertical current wants the kernels' zz and zx terms, which are not built, and the
  attachment of a via to the sheets.
- **The ground's loss.** The ground is taken as perfect, and the solve says so in its notes; phase B's
  Wheeler rule has the ground's share.
- **The metal's loss as a sheet.** The current crowds at a sheet's edges and R grows as the mesh is
  refined, phase B's F4 again. The current also divides equally between the two faces, where a
  microstrip's runs mostly on its bottom face. Phase B's Wheeler R is the better number for a line until
  the thickness is meshed.
- **Low frequencies.** The system's condition grows as 1/w². Small structures are well conditioned down
  to some 10 MHz, and the notes warn when the reciprocal condition falls below 1e-14. A loop-star
  decomposition is not done.
- **Speed.** One dense LU per frequency, single-threaded, with no interpolation over frequency; the
  Green's tables at about 0.1 s a pair of heights a frequency.
- **A metal lid**, refused as before.
