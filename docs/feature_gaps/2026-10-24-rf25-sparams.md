# RF 2.5D, the end of phase B: the extraction as a circuit - S parameters and a Touchstone

The quasi-static rounds gave C, C0, eps_eff, Z0, L, and then R and G. This round is the last line of
the proposal's phase B - *"RLGC, Z0, and S-parameters below the first resonance"* - and the one that
makes the rest worth having: **the extracted part lands in the simulator that is already in front of
you.**

## The round is mostly a crossing, not an invention

The multiphysics engine has turned a line's R, L, G and C into S parameters for some time, for N coupled
lines as well as for one, and has written them as Touchstone, as a SPICE subcircuit and as a Qucs part
(`multiphysics/fem_export.h`, tested against the coupled-line model). What the RF 2.5D extraction
produces *is* an R, L, G and C per metre. So `rf25/rf25_sparams.{h,cpp}` carries it across into the
engine's own `LineRLGC` and `CoupledLines` and then gets out of the way, rather than writing a second
set of writers that could drift from the first.

Three things had to change for the crossing to be honest rather than lossy:

**The loss is a matrix.** `G = w sum(tand_k eps_k dC/deps_k)` was reduced to one number per net. But the
nudged re-solve it comes from produces the *whole* derivative matrix - every pair of nets at once - and a
layer's loss **couples** two conductors as well as loading one. Coupled lines need that term, and the
round that reduced it was throwing away something it had already paid for. `Extraction::dielectricLoss`
keeps it; the per-net `lossTangent` is now derived from its rows, so there is one source of truth.

**The vacuum matrix is kept.** `maxwell0` was computed, converted to the mutual `c0` and discarded. The
coupled relation `L = C0^-1/c^2` needs the short-circuit form, and inverting the mutual form is not the
same matrix.

**There is one mode solver.** The tail of `fem::coupledLines()` - the modes, their currents, their
impedances, the Zc matrix, a symmetric pair's even and odd - is now `fem::coupledModes(lines, w)`, which
both the FEM path and the RF 2.5D path call. An extraction that fills the same matrices by another route
gets the same modes from them; there is no second implementation to diverge.

## What "below the first resonance" actually means

The proposal's phrase is worth unpicking, because it is easy to read it as a limit it is not.

**As a two-port of a given length, a line of a right RLGC is right at any frequency.** The telegrapher's
equations carry its own resonances - a half-wave line is transparent, a quarter-wave line transforms - and
`lineSParameters` gets both from `Zc` and `gamma`. Nothing gives out at the first resonance.

What *does* give out is the **quasi-static cross-section**. A per-metre RLGC is a property of the
cross-section and holds while the cross-section is electrically small; above that a mode other than
quasi-TEM appears and no per-metre model describes it. `quasiStaticLimit()` is that frequency: a tenth of
a wavelength of the larger of how deep the dielectrics go and how wide the widest conductor is, in the
slowest dielectric it crosses. That is the usual engineering rule, and it is reported **as a rule**, with
the dimension that set it, rather than being assumed or hidden:

```
Quasi-static to about 48.2 GHz (the widest conductor is 200 µm across, eps_r 9.8): above that a mode
other than quasi-TEM appears and none of this is the whole story.
This 1200 µm of line is a half wave at 25.4 GHz, which is where its S parameters first turn over.
```

Asking above the limit is answered with the numbers *and* a warning that they are extrapolated, not
solved. That is the one thing a caller must not be left to guess at.

## A sweep costs one solve

C, C0 and L do not move with frequency in a quasi-static extraction. Only R (the skin effect) and G
(`w C tand`) do. So a study's 41 points cost **one** electrostatic solve and 41 cheap evaluations - and
"one frequency at a time", which the previous round listed as not done, is simply gone.

## Where it shows

- **The Results node** adds each net as a two-port into its own impedance, and the quasi-static limit
  with the line's own first resonance.
- **Exports > Touchstone** has a **Write** button, and properties for what to write: one net as a
  two-port, or every net as coupled lines; which net; over what length; into what reference impedance.
  Naming no file it writes `<model>_<tag>.sNp` beside the model.
- **`rf25_solve`** answers S11, S21, S12 and S22 (real, imaginary and in decibels) into an `impedance`
  of its own, the coupled lines' modes and their 2N-port S matrix where there is more than one net, and
  the quasi-static limit with its warning.
- **`rf25_export`** writes the file. It is 135 tools now.

The file's own comments carry what the solve was, each net as a line, and where the quasi-static answer
stops being the whole story - so a Touchstone that outlives this session still says what it is.

## One path to the file

The tab's Write button and `rf25_export` both go through `RF25Doc::exportText(const ExportRequest&)`. The
node is *read into* a request (`exportRequest()`); a tool may give one outright, and its arguments
override what the node says. There is no second path that could answer differently from the panel - the
same rule the solve was wired under, and for the same reason.

## Broken on purpose

`docs/feature_gaps/2026-10-24-rf25-sparams/breaks.py` holds forty-nine, across the matrices kept whole,
the bridge into one line and into coupled lines, the shared mode solver, the sweep, the quasi-static
limit, what is written and the two tools.

## What the breaks taught

Fourteen of the first fifty-one went uncaught, which is the largest share of any round here, and the
reason is worth writing down: **this round's fixtures were lossless and its assertions were ratios**, and
both of those hide things.

- **A lossless fixture cannot tell R from G.** `aNetIsTheEnginesOwnLine` compared every term of the
  crossing into `LineRLGC` - and did it on a stack with no loss, where `R` and `G` are both nothing, so
  swapping them changed not one number. It compares on a lossy stack now, where they differ by three
  orders of magnitude, and `twoLinesAreCoupledLinesWithTheirModes` has a lossy pair beside its lossless
  one for the same reason (each net's resistance was never a number before, so dropping the crowding and
  the length from it was invisible).
- **A lossless line cannot tell `(beta/k0)^2` from `C/C0`.** They are equal there, so the branch that
  chooses between them was unwatched. The test now holds the lossy line's `epsEff` to `(beta/k0)^2`
  *and* asserts it differs from the quasi-static `C/C0`, which is the whole point of the branch.
- **The modes are blind to scale and to symmetry.** `L` is built from `C0`, so dropping the `/length`
  from the matrices left `C0 L = 1/c^2` holding exactly and every `eps_eff` unchanged - the identity I
  was most pleased with could not see a thousandfold error. Only an *absolute* bound can: a microstrip
  pair is some hundreds of pF/m. Symmetrising was likewise invisible to any tolerance, so the test now
  asserts the matrices are symmetric **to the bit**, which is what a generalized self-adjoint
  eigenproblem actually requires.
- **A measurement taken from the thing being measured proves nothing.** `aLossyLinesTransmission-`
  `IsItsAttenuation` compares `|S21|` with `exp(-alpha l)` using the *same* line's `alpha`, so a break
  that changes both stays self-consistent. That test keeps its value as a check of the ABCD conversion,
  but it is no check of the crossing, and the break file now says so rather than listing it.
- **A guard that something else already covers is not a behaviour.** The tool's own "nothing is solved"
  check is backed by `exportText`'s, so removing it still refused - only with a worse message. The test
  now asks for the tool's own wording ("rf25_solve it first"), which is what a tool's caller can act on.
- **Twice one is two.** The tool's coupled port count, `2N`, was exercised only on a one-net model. The
  control test now builds a two-metal stack with no via between them - two nets from the same layout -
  and holds the export to a 4-port, its 4x4 S matrix and its wider rows.

Two breaks were dropped rather than fixed. Skipping the loss solves for a stack that declares none is a
cost guard: the inner loop skips every layer anyway, so the answer is identical either way. And the
refusal of a vacuum capacitance matrix that cannot be inverted is unreachable - no extraction of real
conductors produces a singular one - so it is a guard against a future caller, not a behaviour this
suite can see. Both are said here rather than left in the file as breaks nothing can catch, which is the
same rule the last round used for its own cost guard.

Three others had no test at all when they were written: the study's own spacing (`studySweep` reading
`scale` - `aSweepIsOneSolveAndManyFrequencies` tests `sweepFrequencies` directly and never goes through
the node), a nonsense frequency in a *linear* study (the logarithmic one refuses it for its own reason,
so it masked this), and the guard that clears the modes before filling them again (every caller in the
tree hands it a fresh object). `theStudysOwnFrequenciesAreRead` is new for the first two, and the refusal
test asks for the modes twice for the third.

## What the description cap caught

`rf25_solve` answers a great deal more than it did, and its description grew to 2537 characters against
the 2048 that `theToolsAreListed` holds every tool to. That test is not mine and it earned its keep: a
description over the cap is **cut** at it by the host, so the last third of what the tool says - here,
every word about the quasi-static limit and the warning - would have vanished silently from what a
caller sees. It is 1944 now, and the limit's own explanation lives in the answer's `why` field rather
than being said twice.

## Not done

- **Still no inductance of a loop.** `L = C0^-1/c^2` is exact for a uniform line of any number of
  conductors and says nothing about an inductor. The partial inductances the proposal names need a
  magnetic method of moments and a current distribution, which in turn wants ports - the largest single
  thing left in phase B, and really phase D's company.
- **R has no off-diagonal terms.** One conductor pushing another's current about - the proximity effect -
  is not in an electrostatic solve. The matrix says zero rather than guessing.
- **The current is the charge's shape.** Right for a quasi-TEM line and wrong for a bend, a stub or a
  resonator, where the current has its own distribution. That is what phase C is for.
- **One length for every net.** A layout with two lines of different lengths gets one length for both.
- **No SPICE or Qucs part yet.** `spiceLine()` and `qucsLineSchematic()` take the same `LineRLGC` and
  would be a few lines each, but the proposal puts them in phase F and this round stays in phase B.
- **Mixed-mode Touchstone is written single-ended.** `touchstoneCoupled` has a mixed-mode form; the
  export always asks for the single-ended one.
- **Nothing is kept with the document.** A solve, and so an export, lives in the tab until the next edit.
