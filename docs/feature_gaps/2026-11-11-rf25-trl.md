# RF 2.5D: a multiline TRL calibration, the pads taken out

The request: "Build F3 next", one of the open items of 8 November
([2026-11-08-claude-rf25-open-items.md](../bug_hunts/2026-11-08-claude-rf25-open-items.md)): GSG pad de-embedding and a
multiline TRL-style calibration.

The strips' calibration (`rf25_deembed.h`) takes out a port's own face, found from two lengths of its feed. A pad at a
port is no part of that. A probe's landing, with its own fringing and its taper to the line, is a fixture, and what a
fixture is gets told by standards made with it, as on a wafer.

## What the model takes

There is a new **TRL Calibration** node under Ports, one at most. It names three kinds of standard. Each is a model
(.qrf25) of its own, relative to this model's folder, with the same fixtures:

- **Thru:** the two fixtures joined. The line between them is as long as the fixture's feed on each side of the device.
- **Lines:** rows of a model and how much longer than the thru it is ("500[um]"). One at least. Lengths whose phases
  differ by about a quarter wave tell best.
- **Reflect:** the two fixtures each ended alike, near an open or near a short (its own setting).

The node also takes **the lines' impedance** (optional). Given it, the calibrated S is stated in the ports' references.

The model's problems name a calibration missing its thru, its reflect or its lines, and one on a model that is not a
two-port.

## What a solve does

A full-wave solve of the model goes on, on its thread, to solve each standard as it is:
- the standard's model is read, its layout read and processed, its mesh made with its own settings;
- its ports are taken from the model, as its own tab would;
- it is solved at this model's frequencies, raw.

It then calibrates (`rf25_trl.*`) and corrects the structure's raw S. The ports' own de-embedding, where they ask for
it, is not taken: the error boxes hold their faces along with the fixtures, and the answer says so.

A calibration wants every frequency solved, its standards at each, so it refuses the adaptive sweep. A mesh convergence
check takes no calibration.

**The 8-term error model** (Engen and Hoer, 1979) is worked in wave-cascading matrices, [b1; a1] = T [a2; b2]. What is
solved is T_A T T_B. Of the thru and a line longer by l:

    T_L T_T⁻¹ = T_A diag(e^-γl, e^γl) T_A⁻¹,   T_T⁻¹ T_L = T_B⁻¹ diag(e^-γl, e^γl) T_B

So A's columns and B's rows are the eigenvectors, which give e00, e33, e01e10/e11 and e23e32/e22. The rest follows:
- the thru gives e11e22 (from each end, averaged) and e10e32;
- the reflect gives e11 and e22, its sign told by whether it is near an open or a short.

**With several lines** (Marks, 1991, whose Gauss–Markov weighting this simplifies):
- each frequency takes its eigenvectors from the line whose phase beside the thru's is furthest from 0 and 180°;
- the propagation constant comes from all the lines, each weighted by that distance and by its length, its phase
  unwrapped from the frequency below;
- the lines not taken, corrected, are a check of how near a matched line each comes.

**The corrected S** is at the thru's middle: the device's model is the fixture with the thru's half on each side. It is
in the lines' own impedance, which no calibration of lines alone can know. Given that impedance, S is renormalized to
the ports' references; without it, a warning says so.

**What it says:**
- the lines' effective permittivity and loss at the top of the sweep, and the reflect as found there;
- how near a matched line the other lines come;
- a band where no line's phase is more than 12° from 0 or 180° (add a line of another length);
- a reflect far from reflecting all.

## Checks

**On fixtures made up in closed form** (`test_rf25_trl`): two error boxes unlike each other, a lossy line and an open.
The calibration gets back the device behind them (a series R and L, a shunt G and C) to 1e-8, the lines' γ to 1e-6, and
the reflect to 1e-8.
- **A line exactly half a wave long at 20 GHz:** that frequency takes the other line, and the device is found all the
  same. Alone, that line's phase there is said to tell nothing.
- **A short reflect:** told by its kind. Taken for an open, it does not give the device back.

**On RF 2.5D's own solves** (`test_rf25_doc`). The fixture is a 150 µm pad and 100 µm of 50 µm feed on 100 µm of
alumina. The standards are a thru, lines 500 and 1500 µm longer, and an open reflect. The device is an 800 µm line
between the fixtures:

| | 5 GHz | 15 GHz | 25 GHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| \|S11\| as solved | 0.0645 | 0.185 | 0.296 |
| \|S11\| calibrated | 0.00018 | 0.00062 | 0.0015 |
| \|S21\| calibrated | 0.9922 | 0.9907 | 0.9897 |
| S21's phase off the calibration's γ over 800 µm | 3e-4 rad | 6e-4 rad | 5e-4 rad |

- The lines' effective permittivity comes out at 6.31 and their loss at 0.11 dB/mm. The |S21| below one is that loss
  over 0.8 mm.
- The line not taken comes within 0.0016 of a matched line.

## Where it is

- `rf25/rf25_trl.*` (new): the calibration, the correction, the renormalization, and the cascading matrices.
- `rf25/rf25_model.cpp`: the TRL Calibration node and the model's problems with it.
- `rf25doc.*`: the standards read from the node (`SolveJob::Trl`), each solved on the solve's thread
  (`solvedStandard()`), and the structure calibrated (`trlOn()`).

[breaks.py](2026-11-11-rf25-trl/breaks.py) undoes each piece on purpose, builds and runs its tests:
[results.txt](2026-11-11-rf25-trl/results.txt). All 15 are caught.

One was not caught at first: the reflect taken for a short when it is an open. A symmetric device's S is the same
whichever it is taken for, because both error boxes' signs turn together. The calibration now says the reflect it
found, and the pads' test holds it near an open.

## What it does not do

- **Two ports alone,** an 8-term model: no switch terms, no leakage between the ports past the error boxes.
- **The lines' impedance is the user's to give.** Without it, the calibrated S is in the lines' own. No Marks–Williams
  estimate from a capacitance per length is made.
- **The weighting is simpler than Marks's:** the best-told line's eigenvectors at each frequency, rather than a
  Gauss–Markov combination of all of them. Its γ does come from all of them.
- **The standards are solved again** with every solve of the structure, not kept beside their own models. Each is small
  beside a structure, but each is a full-wave solve at every frequency.
- **The standards' calibrated S is not shown,** nor the error boxes themselves.
