# RF 2.5D, phase B: what a line loses - R from the metal, G from the dielectrics

The quasi-static rounds gave C, C0 and from them eps_eff, Z0 and L. This is the other half of the
proposal's phase B line - "R from the surface impedance ... RLGC" - and it is the half that makes the
numbers useful at a frequency rather than at zero.

## R: the surface impedance, and where the current actually is

Two pieces, and the second is the one worth having.

**The surface impedance** of the metal is that of a *slab*, not a half space:

```
Zs = (1 + j)/(sigma d) coth((1 + j) t/d),     d = sqrt(2/(w mu sigma))
```

which tends to `1/(sigma d)` where the metal is many skin depths thick and to the direct-current
`1/(sigma t)` where it is thin beside one. A half-space formula gets the first right and the second badly
wrong, and thin metal is the common case on a chip. The `coth` is written so that a thick metal comes out
as the 1 it tends to rather than as a ratio of two runaway exponentials.

**Where the current is** is the part a solver can do and a formula cannot. `R = Rs/W` assumes the current
is spread evenly across the strip; it is not - it crowds at the edges, and that is most of why a real line
loses more than the textbook says. On a quasi-TEM line the current follows the charge, and the charge is
exactly what the electrostatic solve already found. So the extraction keeps, per net,

```
crowding = sum(q_i^2 / A_i) / (sum q_i)^2        [1/m^2]
```

with the net held at a volt, and `R = Rs x length x crowding`. For a strip of width W whose current runs
evenly that comes to `Rs/W` identically, which is the check that it is the right quantity; where the
charge crowds it is larger, and the resistance with it. Measured on a 200 µm strip over 100 µm of alumina
at 10 GHz: between one and four times `Rs/W`, which is the edge crowding and nothing else.

## G: each dielectric's share of the field

A stack's loss is not one tan delta. The energy a layer holds is `eps dC/deps`, so

```
G = w sum_k (tand_k eps_k dC/deps_k)
```

and `dC/deps_k` is taken by solving again with that layer's permittivity nudged a part in a thousand - one
extra solve per *lossy* layer, and none at all for a stack that declares no loss. A microstrip whose
substrate has `tand = 0.002` comes out with an effective loss tangent between half of that and all of it,
which is the share of its field that is in the substrate rather than in the air above: exactly the
quantity that makes a dielectric's loss into a line's.

## And what the four make

With R, L, G and C there is a line:

```
Zc = sqrt((R + jwL)/(G + jwC)),   gamma = sqrt((R + jwL)(G + jwC)),   alpha in dB/m
```

with the root taken on the side that loses. The lossless `Z0` stays beside them - a good line's `Zc` is
within a few per cent of it, and the difference is the loss.

Where no frequency is asked for, R and G are nothing and the quasi-static values stand alone, which is
what the earlier rounds answered and still answer.

## Where it shows

- **The tab** reports RLGC at the top of the model's own sweep, beside the capacitance it already said:
  `M1 at 40 GHz: R 112 Ohm/m, L 318 nH/m, G 0.0021 S/m, C 170 pF/m; 4.7 dB/m`.
- **`rf25_solve`** takes a `frequency` of its own (the sweep's top when it is not given) and answers `R`,
  `G`, the skin depth, the surface resistance, `Zc`, `alpha` and `beta` with the rest.

## Broken on purpose

`docs/feature_gaps/2026-10-24-rf25-rlgc/breaks.py` holds seventeen: a metal taken as a half space however
thin; the thick-metal `coth` left as a ratio of runaways; the permeability dropped from the skin depth; a
metal with no conductivity given one; the charge taken as even however it crowds; the crowding of a charge
rather than a density; the length dropped from R; every dielectric's loss counted the same; a lossless
stack solved again for nothing; G not `w C tand`; `Zc` of the series arm alone; `gamma` from a sum rather
than a product; the root taken on the gaining side; nepers reported as decibels; a frequency of none taken
as one; and the three places it is said going quiet.

## What the breaks taught

Five of the first eighteen went uncaught, and four of them were tests that did not reach far enough:

- **Sixty skin depths is not thick enough to overflow anything.** The guard that returns 1 for a thick
  metal's `coth` exists because `cosh` and `sinh` of a large argument both run away - but `cosh(60)` is
  5.7e25, which a double holds without complaint, so the test never reached the case the guard is for.
  Two thousand skin depths does overflow, and the test now asks for that and for a finite answer.
- **A bound is not a relation.** `alpha` in decibels was held only to being positive and under 500, which
  nepers also are. It is held to `20/ln(10)` times `gamma`'s real part now, which is what the conversion
  *is*.
- **Look for what the break actually changes, not for what it sounds like it would.** Taking the
  frequency guard out leaves R and G at zero, `Zc` and `gamma` at zero (the division is already guarded
  against a shunt of nothing) and `alpha` at zero: everything the test asked about was the same with the
  guard gone. The one thing that *does* change is the skin depth, `sqrt(2/(w mu sigma))`, which at no
  frequency is **infinite** - and the test had never looked at it. My first strengthening of this test
  asserted that `Zc` was zero rather than NaN, which was a guess at the failure rather than a reading of
  it, and the break went uncaught a second time. The test holds the skin depth and the surface
  resistance to nothing now, which is what a line asked for nothing at no frequency has.
- **The tab's RLGC line needs a metal that conducts.** The test's model had no conductivity, so the line
  was never printed and rewriting it changed nothing visible. The model is given copper and a lossy
  substrate now, and the line is read back at the study's own 40 GHz.

The fifth is a cost guard, not a behaviour: skipping the extra solve for a dielectric that declares no
loss gives the same answer either way, only faster. It carries no break, as the other cost-only measures
in these rounds do not, and this says so rather than leaving an uncatchable one in the file.

## Not done

- **No inductance of a loop.** `L = 1/(c0^2 C0)` is exact for a uniform line and says nothing about an
  inductor. The partial inductances the proposal names need a second, magnetic method of moments and a
  current distribution to go with it - which in turn wants ports, since a loop's current is what a port
  drives. That is the largest single thing left in phase B, and it is really phase D's company.
- **No S-parameters yet.** RLGC is what they are made from, and a two-port line's S from `Zc` and `gamma`
  is a short step; a Touchstone export is the step after.
- **R is of the signal conductor alone.** The ground plane's own loss, and a return path that is not a
  plane, are not counted.
- **The current is the charge's shape.** That is right for a quasi-TEM line and wrong for anything that
  is not one - a bend, a stub, a resonator - where the current has its own distribution.
- **One frequency at a time.** The sweep in the model's study is read for its top only; a line's R and G
  over a band is the next thing a user will want.
