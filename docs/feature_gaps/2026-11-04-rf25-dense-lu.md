# RF 2.5D: its two large dense LUs on Accelerate's LAPACK

Approved by the user on 4 November ("Yes, switch them"), after a benchmark of dense LU on the M2 Ultra in a side
session: `rf25-dense-lu-benchmark.md` and its programs in the side-session notes folder of 4 November
(`~/Library/Application Support/Claude/side-session-notes/local_35510ace-…/rf25-dense-lu-bench/`). RF 2.5D factorized
two large dense systems with Eigen's `PartialPivLU`, single-threaded:

- **`rf25_fullwave.cpp`'s `solveAt`**: the complex system of a frequency's runs of current, runs × runs: about 4000 at
  the dense limit (`kMostUnknowns`, 6000 runs and cells).
- **`rf25_static.cpp`'s charge solve**: the real potential coefficients of the quasi-static extraction's cells, up to
  `kMostCharges` (8000), once with the stack and once in vacuum (and once more for each lossy layer of a non-uniform
  layout).

On macOS 13.3 and later both are now factorized by Accelerate's LAPACK, its new interface (`ACCELERATE_NEW_LAPACK`), in
`rf25/rf25_dense.{h,cpp}`. Elsewhere, before 13.3, and when asked, they are factorized by Eigen as before. The other dense
LUs stay on Eigen. They are the full wave's port-sized ones (`:1710`, `:2568`, `:2570`, `:2687`) and the static
cross-sections' (`rf25_static.cpp` `:469`, `:813`), each a few dozen to a few hundred rows.

**In the app**, through `rf25_solve` on three of the reviewers' models, one frequency at 10 GHz (the minimum of six,
the two apps in turn):

| model (runs + cells) | before (77fad7a) | now | its LU, Eigen → Accelerate | its fill |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `fgc_S51_L2000_r1p8` (1468 + 740) | 0.80 s | 0.65 s | 0.199 → 0.043 s | 0.31 s |
| `ms30_L1000_m10` (2334 + 1224) | 1.71 s | 1.12 s | 0.766 → 0.147 s | 0.41 s |
| `fgc_S62_L1000_r15p6_c10` (3588 + 1800) | 5.72 s | 3.36 s | 2.645 → 0.322 s | 1.57 → 1.62 s |

- **The largest model's whole sweep**, 40 frequencies three at a time, went from 75.6 s to 44.2 s.
- **Its peak memory** went from 2682 MB to 1119 MB, and for one frequency from 1179 MB to 578 MB. Most of that is not the
  copy no longer made: Eigen's `rcond()` on a copied `PartialPivLU` copies the whole LU twice more (below).
- **A quasi-static solve of 5628 charges** (`ms30_L1000_m2.5`) went from 16.5 s to 10.4 s.
- **Every rf25 suite passes** in Release and under ASan. The full ctest passes but for a paint timing that passed
  alone. The changed functions also pass with `QUCS_FEM_SOLVER=eigen` and `QUCS_RF25_LU` set either way.

## What it is

- **`DenseFactors<Scalar>`** (complex or real) takes the matrix and factorizes it where it is. The full wave's `m` and the
  extraction's `p` are not needed after their LU, so neither is copied, where Eigen's `PartialPivLU(m)` copied it. The
  Eigen path is in place too, through `PartialPivLU<Ref<Matrix>>`.
- **The complex LU is blocked 256 columns at a time.** Each panel is factorized by `zgetrf`. Its row swaps go to the
  columns on either side by `zlaswp`, U12 by `ztrsm` and the trailing matrix by `zgemm`: Eigen's own blocked algorithm,
  made of LAPACK's pieces. In the benchmark, Accelerate's `zgetrf` on the whole matrix was 6.4 times Eigen at 4000 rows
  and 7.5 at 6000; this blocked LU was 9.3 and 11.2. A panel of 128 was a quarter slower at 4000, and one of 512 within
  7 % either way. The real LU is `dgetrf` on the whole matrix: 14.6 times Eigen at 6000, 17 at 8000.
- **A solve of fewer than eight columns is padded to eight.** Accelerate's `zgetrs` of two columns took three times its
  solve of eight in the benchmark (33 against 10 ms at 4000 complex rows), and Eigen's two took 23. Padded, a two- or
  three-port solve is faster than Eigen's, not slower. The quasi-static solve now solves every net at once, where it
  solved each net in turn.
- **rcond** is LAPACK's `gecon`, given the matrix's 1-norm, taken before the LU; a pivot of nought reads 0. The full
  wave's near-singular warning (`rcond < 1e-14`) reads it as it read Eigen's. In the tests `gecon` gave the true rcond
  (`1/(‖A‖₁ ‖A⁻¹‖₁)`, A⁻¹ formed whole), and Eigen's estimate was 1.04 to 1.32 times it.
- **The new interface is macOS 13.3's**, and the release is built for 13.0. Every call is inside
  `__builtin_available(macOS 13.3, *)`, with Eigen the fallback. The classic interface is never the fallback, although
  13.0 has it: in the benchmark, for a complex matrix it was half the new one's speed and peaked at three times the
  matrix's memory. Accelerate stays weakly linked, through `fem`'s `-weak_framework Accelerate`.
  `test_macos_accelerate_weak` passes, and the nine new imports (`zgetrf$NEWLAPACK`, `zlaswp`, `ztrsm`, `zgemm`, `zgetrs`,
  `zgecon`, and `dgetrf`, `dgetrs`, `dgecon`) are weak in the object built for 13.0 and in the packaged app.
- **The build** turns it on where the SDK has the new interface: `QUCS_ACCELERATE_LAPACK` on `rf25/rf25_dense.cpp`
  alone, from a `check_cxx_source_compiles` in `qucs/CMakeLists.txt`, as `fem_sparse.cpp`'s `QUCS_ACCELERATE` is set.
  Linux and Windows compile only the Eigen path. Nothing in it is new to GCC: no long lambdas, and no tuple returned as a
  pair.
- **Threading**: Accelerate's default, which in the benchmark was never slower than its single-threaded setting
  (`BLASSetThreading`, macOS 15 and per thread), at 1 to 24 frequencies at a time.
- **Choosing** follows `fem_sparse.cpp`'s pattern:
  - `setDenseLu(DenseLu::Automatic | Eigen | Accelerate)` for the process; the tests use it to run both paths.
  - **`QUCS_RF25_LU`** (`auto`, `eigen` or `accelerate`) overrides it.
  - `QUCS_FEM_SOLVER=eigen` does too. That keeps Eigen everywhere, as on CI's Linux, for timing a sanitized build:
    Accelerate is not instrumented.
  - `accelerateLuCount()` and `eigenLuCount()` count each one's factorizations.
- **What it says.** The full wave times each frequency's fill (from its start to its LU) and its LU (the factors, the
  solve and rcond), and `FullWave` sums them and says whether Accelerate factorized. `rf25_solve`'s `matrix` answer for
  a dense solve now carries `"LU by"` (`Accelerate` or `Eigen`), with `"fill s"` and `"LU s"` per frequency: what the
  in-app timings below were read from.
- **Left as they were**, as asked: `kMostUnknowns` (6000) and the sweep's memory per frequency,
  `16 (2 nr² + nc²) + 1e6` bytes against `kSweepMemory` (1.5 GB). What the numbers support is below.

## In the app

**How it was run.**
- The app's MCP server was driven headless: `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen`, with home, settings, cache and trash in a
  scratch folder, and nothing written to `~/Library/Caches/qucs-s` or the workspace (checked with `find -newer`).
- Three of the reviewers' models from `~/QucsWorkspace` (`bench_ponchak_fgc`, `rf25_check`) were copied, each with its
  layout and without its kept solution, into a fresh folder for every solve.
- `rf25_solve` was called with `solver: full_wave`, `matrix: dense` and one frequency at 10 GHz, then with the model's
  own sweep, and with `solver: quasi_static`.
- The apps:
  - **before** was 77fad7a's Release build;
  - **now** was 77fad7a with this change, built the same way in a worktree;
  - and now again with `QUCS_RF25_LU=eigen`, Eigen's LU in place.
- The three were run in turn, two rounds of three solves each, on a machine where other sessions used one to three
  cores (load 5 to 9).
- The first solve in a process builds the stack's Green's functions (fill 1.5 s there, 0.31 s after), so the minimum
  is the steady state.

**One frequency** (minimum of six; fill = the Green's functions, the system and P up to the LU; LU = the factors, the
solve and rcond):

| model | runs + cells | before | now: fill + LU | now, Eigen asked: fill + LU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fgc_S51_L2000_r1p8 | 1468 + 740 | 0.803 s | 0.646 s: 0.312 + 0.043 | 0.784 s: 0.305 + 0.199 |
| ms30_L1000_m10 | 2334 + 1224 | 1.707 s | 1.122 s: 0.415 + 0.147 | 1.715 s: 0.403 + 0.766 |
| fgc_S62_L1000_r15p6_c10 | 3588 + 1800 | 5.716 s | 3.362 s: 1.624 + 0.322 | 5.593 s: 1.569 + 2.645 |

- **Eigen in place reads as before did**, within 2 %, so its split is the before split.
- **The LU was 63 % of a frequency's fill and LU** on the largest model, not the 40 % estimated from the code's own
  8 s. It is 17 % now, 4.6 to 8.2 times quicker. The rest of a solve (its mesh, the static inductances and potentials
  made once) is the same in all three: 1.4 to 1.5 s on the largest.
- **The whole sweep**: 40 frequencies, three at a time (`side`, from `kSweepMemory`). Before, 75.6 s. Now, 44.2 s
  (fill 2.65 s and LU 0.44 s a frequency, on its thread). Eigen asked, 77.4 s (fill 2.67 s and LU 2.76 s). Three fills
  at once take 1.6 times one alone: they share the memory.
- **The quasi-static solve of `ms30_L1000_m2.5`, 5628 charges**: before 16.5 s, now 10.4 s. That is two LUs (the stack
  and vacuum) of some 3.1 s each with Eigen. The 1800 charges of `fgc_S62` went 14.3 s either way: its Green's
  functions are the time.
- **Solves of the full wave's ports** (a probe linked to `rf25_dense.cpp`, minimum of 15). The solve of 1, 2 or 3 ports
  now costs what its solve of 8 does, padded, and less than Eigen's:

  | complex rows | Eigen, 2 / 3 ports | Accelerate, 2 / 3 ports |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2000 | 6.2 / 8.2 ms | 3.6 / 3.6 ms |
  | 4000 | 23.8 / 30.0 ms | 10.1 / 10.4 ms |
  | 6000 | 52.7 / 64.8 ms | 21.9 / 21.9 ms |

**Memory** (`/usr/bin/time -l`'s peak footprint of the whole app process):

| solve | before | now | now, Eigen asked |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ms30_L1000_m10, one frequency | 551 MB | 304 MB | |
| fgc_S62, one frequency | 1179 MB | 578 MB | 580 MB |
| fgc_S62, its 40-frequency sweep, three at a time | 2682 MB | 1119 MB | |

- **What the old code held.** It copied the system into its LU, and its `lu.rcond()` copied the LU twice more: Eigen's
  estimator solves with `dec.adjoint()`, whose expression holds a copied `PartialPivLU` by value. In a probe of 2500
  complex rows (95 MiB), the footprint went 96 MiB with the matrix, then 203 with the LU, then 394 after `rcond()`, and
  it stayed there.
- **What the new code holds.** `PartialPivLU<Ref<...>>` copies only a reference, and `gecon` nothing (107, 107, 107 MiB).
  A frequency held four of the runs × runs matrix at its peak; it holds one now.
- **The measured figures fit that.** The base app is about 90 MB and the parts made once per solve
  16 nr² + 8 nc² = 232 MB. A frequency is now 16 (nr² + nc²) = 258 MB: 578 MB measured for one (90 + 232 + 258), and
  (1119 − 322) / 3 = 266 MB each for three. It was about 16 (3.6 nr² + nc²): (2682 − 322) / 3 = 787 MB.

**What the numbers support** (not changed: the user's call):

- **The memory term.** A frequency is `16 (nr² + nc²) + 1e6` bytes now, where `kSweepMemory`'s
  `16 (2 nr² + nc²) + 1e6` assumes the system and an LU copy. (The old code's peak was nearer `16 (3.6 nr² + nc²)`:
  the term understated it about twice, and at the dense limit two frequencies held about 2 GB against the budget's
  1.5.) With the term as measured, the limit's case (6000 runs and cells, some 4000 runs and 2000 cells) is 321 MB a
  frequency, so four at a time instead of two.
- **`kMostUnknowns`.** (Superseded the same day by `2026-11-04-rf25-settings.md`: against the compressed solve, not
  the dense one alone, past 6000 the compressed one was 7 to 17 times the quicker on microstrip and about even on a
  coplanar line of narrow gaps, so Automatic stays 6000 and RF 2.5D > Settings… sets another.)
  A frequency at today's limit took, before, about 1.57 × (6000/5388)² + 2.645 × (6000/5388)³
  = 5.6 s alone: the fill as N², the LU as nr³, runs two thirds of the unknowns as here. Now the same 5.6 s is reached
  at about 8700 runs and cells (fill 4.2 s, LU 1.4 s), with Accelerate's LU taken as cubic. That is conservative: its
  rate still grew past 8000 in the benchmark. There the term measured is 673 MB a frequency, two at a time, where the
  term as it stands (1.21 GB) allows one. So a limit of about 8500 costs no more time a frequency than 6000 did.
  Beyond it the single-threaded fill is the time (three quarters of a frequency at 8700). The fill as N² is an
  extrapolation from one model, and where the compressed solve overtakes a larger dense one was not measured.

## Tests

- **`test_rf25_dense`, new.** Each path is checked against the matrix and the two against each other. On Linux and
  Windows Eigen's is checked alone.

  | function | what it checks |
  |---|---|
  | `aRandomMatrixIsSolvedAlike` | Complex and real matrices of 900 rows (600 sanitized): the blocked LU's three panels of 256 and a part, rows swapped throughout. Each solution has a backward error under 1e-14 (4e-16 to 2.4e-15 measured). The two agree to 1e-11 (3e-13 measured). Each rcond is at or above the true one and within three times it, and the two within a factor of two (`gecon` gave the true one, Eigen 1.04 to 1.32 times it). |
  | `aMomentMatrixIsSolvedAlike` | The same on matrices shaped as a method of moments': complex symmetric, the coupling decaying as exp(-jkr)/r, diagonally dominant, no row swapped. They agree to 1e-13 (4e-15 measured). |
  | `anyNumberOfRightHandSidesIsSolved` | 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9 and 20 right-hand sides, so the padding and past it; a matrix of one row; one of 256 rows, a panel exactly. |
  | `aSingularMatrixIsSaidToBe` | A column of noughts: the rcond never reads at or above the warning's 1e-14, either way. |
  | `theEnvironmentChoosesOverTheProcess` | `setDenseLu`, `QUCS_RF25_LU` over it (any case, spaces trimmed), and `QUCS_FEM_SOLVER=eigen` under `QUCS_RF25_LU`. |
  | `macOSFactorizesWithAccelerate` | On macOS 13.3 and later the build has turned Accelerate on, and an LU of Automatic is Accelerate's. Elsewhere it has not. |

- **`test_rf25_fullwave::theSystemIsFactorizedAlikeEitherWay`.** A line on alumina is solved by Eigen and by
  Automatic: the same S to 1e-10 and the currents to 1e-9, whose LU it was said, the fill's and the LU's seconds above
  nought, and one factorization a frequency counted.
- **`test_rf25_static::theChargesAreSolvedAlikeEitherWay`.** Three strips are solved both ways: the same Maxwell
  matrices with the stack and in vacuum to 1e-12, one factorization a solve, the condition hint the same.
- **`test_qucs_control::anRf25ModelIsSolvedFullWaveThroughItsTool`.** It now reads `"LU by"`, `"fill s"` and `"LU s"`
  in `rf25_solve`'s dense answer.
- **Overrides cleared.** The two functions that choose a path clear `QUCS_RF25_LU` and `QUCS_FEM_SOLVER` while they run
  and put them back. A suite run with `QUCS_FEM_SOLVER=eigen`, to time it the CI way, then still passes.
