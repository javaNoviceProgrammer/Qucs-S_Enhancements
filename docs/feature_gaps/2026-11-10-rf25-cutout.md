# RF 2.5D's Cutout: solving a part of a layout

The request: "Sometimes I have a full layout, but I want to only simulate parts of the layout in RF2.5D. Can you add a new
node called "Cutout" to the RF2.5D tree such that we can add sub-nodes to it via a right click item called "Add Region". A
cutout sub-node can define a 2D region (rectangle, polygon, ...) that the RF 2.5D layout processor applies and masks the rest
of the regions that are defined."

## What was built

- **The Cutout** (`rf_cutout`, tag `cutout`) sits under Layout, beside the Layer map and Processing. Like them it is a fixed
  part of the tree. Every new model has one, and so does a file written before it (made when the file is read). Its one
  property, **Apply its regions** (on by default), switches all of its regions off at once: the whole layout is then solved,
  and the regions are kept for later.
- **Add Region** is in the Cutout's right-click menu itself, not under an Add submenu of one item. It is also a button in
  the Cutout's form under the tree, as the Stack's items were made buttons of its form on 10 November.
- **A Region** (`rf_region`) is one of three shapes:
  - **a rectangle**, by two corners (From x, From y, To x, To y), in either order;
  - **a polygon**, by its corners as a table of x and y, three at least (a last corner repeating the first is not counted);
  - **a circle**, by its centre and radius, drawn as a polygon whose sides are within a nanometre of the circle (32 to 1024
    sides).

  Each length is an expression in the layout's coordinates, in metres (`30[um]`, or a parameter's name), as a port's x
  and y are. Only the shape's own lengths are shown in its form.
- **What is inside a region** is either **kept, the rest masked** (the default) or **taken out**. On each layer, what the
  regions that keep hold is kept (the whole layer when none keeps it), and then what the regions that take out hold is cut
  away. Overlapping regions that keep act as their union: a shape across two of them is kept whole.
- **On layers** names the stack layers a region cuts, separated by commas (empty: every layer). Its "…" menu takes each of
  the stack's metals and vias in or out, and renaming a layer renames it in the regions too.
- A region can be disabled, renamed, duplicated, moved and deleted like any node a user adds. Each change is one Undo step.
- **In the tree's second column**, the Cutout says "the whole layout", "N region(s)" or "not applied", and a region says
  "kept" or "taken out".
- **In the tab**, the regions are drawn over the layout whatever else is shown: each region that keeps as an outline, each
  that takes out tinted as well.
- **The control tools** need nothing new: the generic node tools add and set an `rf_region` as they do any RF 2.5D node.
  `fem_describe what=format` for an RF 2.5D model now describes `rf_cutout` and `rf_region`.

## Where it acts

The layout's shapes are cut in `processShapes()` (`rf25/rf25_layout.cpp`) before every processing step. It runs on each
layer after dummy fill is removed and before healing, snapping, merging and dropping slivers. So:

- What a region masks is never healed, merged or meshed, which on a whole chip's layout is most of the work.
- A shape across a region's edge is cut along it. If the cut leaves a sliver, the processing's own sliver step drops it.
- Everything that takes the processed shapes sees only what is left: the mesh, both solvers, the edge-port and terminal
  tools that find a conductor's face, and the HFSS™ 3D Layout export.

`cutoutOf(model, problems)` reads the regions (in µm), and `cutOf(shapes, cutout, layer)` cuts a layer's shapes.
`processRead()` reads the Cutout into `Processing::cutout`, so a test can hand `processShapes()` regions it made itself.

Each shape is cut on its own, never together with the other shapes of its layer. So a cut never joins two shapes: with
the merge turned off, two overlapping shapes inside a region stay two. A shape whose bounding box misses every region that
keeps is dropped without a boolean. A shape that a region holds whole is handed on as it was drawn, with the same points in
the same order, not as the boolean redrew it. The test for "whole" is that the cut changed its area by no more than the
boolean's 1 nm grid could, along its whole boundary.

Overlapping regions that keep are the boolean's one operand, so their union is what keeps. gdstk counts a point as inside
wherever the windings around it do not sum to zero, which would make a clockwise polygon over an anticlockwise one cancel
where they overlap. It does not happen, because gdstk turns every polygon anticlockwise before its boolean. The test of two
overlapping regions that keep gives one of them clockwise, so a gdstk that stopped doing so would be caught.

## What it says

- **Under Processing** (and the tab's report): "The cutout's N region(s) applied first." Then, for each layer it changed:
  "M1: the cutout cut 3 shape(s) along its regions' edges and masked 40 other(s)."
- **Under the Cutout:** how many shapes were cut and masked; "No region: the whole layout is solved."; or "Not applied: the
  whole layout is solved."
- **A region that cannot be read** is named and left out, and the problem is said under the Cutout and among the
  processing's problems. That covers a length that is no number, a polygon of fewer than three corners, a region of no area,
  a circle of no radius, and a layer that is not in the stack.
- **When the regions keep none of the shapes**, it says so ("The cutout's regions keep none of the shapes … Their lengths are
  the layout's coordinates, in metres: 30[um]"), not the plain "Every shape was dropped". A region typed in the wrong unit is
  the likeliest cause.

## Not done

- A port outside what the Cutout keeps sits on no conductor. The solve then says so, as for any port off its metal; the
  Cutout itself does not check the ports.
- Regions are typed in, not drawn on the layout. A tool that drags a rectangle on the view into a new region would be the
  natural next step, as the Internal Port tool drags a port's rectangle.

## Tests

- **`test_rf25_layout`:**
  - `everyModelHasACutout`: a new model, a file of before it, the regions written and read back, a layer renamed in them,
    and the Region going under the Cutout and nowhere else.
  - `theCutoutIsReadFromItsRegions`: each shape in µm through a parameter; corners in either order; a closing corner; the
    circle within a nanometre; a disabled region; the Cutout not applied; and each thing that cannot be read, said by name.
  - `theCutoutKeepsWhatIsInsideAndMasksTheRest`: inside kept as drawn, across cut, outside masked; a hole taken out of a
    plane; two overlapping regions of opposite winding; another layer's region.
  - `theProcessingCutsFirst`: the counts, the lines, the merge off and on, and nothing left blamed on the Cutout.
  - `aLayoutFileIsCutByTheModelsCutout`: the multiphysics heater example's layout cut to its left half by a model's
    Cutout through `processRead()`, turned off, and given a region that cannot be read.
- **`test_rf25_doc`'s `aCutoutSolvesAPartOfTheLayout`:**
  - Add Region from the menu and from the form's button, each one Undo step.
  - The status column.
  - The region's form showing a rectangle's corners or a polygon's table, and the layers picker.
  - The shapes and the mesh of the tab inside the region.
  - The outline drawn with the shapes and ports hidden.
  - The Cutout's report, and the Cutout turned off.

[breaks.py](2026-11-10-rf25-cutout/breaks.py) undoes each piece on purpose, builds and runs its tests. All 22 breaks are
caught: [results.txt](2026-11-10-rf25-cutout/results.txt). Its first run caught 22 of 23. The one it missed was a winding
step that did nothing, now taken out. That run also found the check of the Cutout turned off too weak (it counted the shapes
but not their area), and the check was made stronger.
