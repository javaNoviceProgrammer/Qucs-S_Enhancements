# RF 2.5D, phase C: vias

`1db0e32` wired the full-wave solve into the tab, but it refused any layout with a via: "its current runs up
and down, which the full-wave solve does not do yet". The user said "Carry on with vias" (1 November), the
last piece of the proposal's phase C: "vertical attachment modes for vias". A via is now solved, between two
metals and down to the ground plane, which the stack now allows.

## What it does now

- **A via is a run of current in z.** It runs from the cell of the metal under it to the cell of the metal
  over it, and charges them as any run charges its ends. The via's own cells that land on the same two cells
  are one run: their current goes the same way, and a via cut ten across would otherwise be a hundred runs.
  A via carries no charge of its own, as in phase B: in the thin-metal approximation its sidewall's charge is
  small beside the sheets'.
- **A via down to the ground.** The stack lets a via join the lowest metal to the ground plane, named
  "ground" (as an internal port names it) or by the ground's own name. Its foot has no node: the ground
  takes its charge, at nought.
- **Formulation C's two kernels for a vertical current** (`rf25_wave`'s `ViaGreen`, Michalski and Zheng's
  formulation, which the horizontal kernels are of):
  - G_zz, the vector potential of a vertical current, `(mu0/(j w eps')) I_v^e`, integrated along both vias:
    the coupling of one via's current to the voltage along the other.
  - The correction a vertical current makes to the scalar potential. Its source derivative is
    `(w^2 mu0/kr^2)(V_v^e - V_v^h)`, and integrated along the via it gives the potential, Ohm, that a unit
    current up the via adds at a metal height.
- **Both integrated along whole slabs.** A via always spans whole slabs, its metals sitting on their faces,
  so both integrals come down to the voltage of a shunt source between the slabs' faces. That is the same
  `lineVoltage()` the horizontal kernels use, and the double integral along two vias is a second difference
  of it:
  `Int_n Int_m I_v = (delta_nm d_n - D^2 V_i / (j kz_m Z_m)) / (j kz_n Z_n)`.
- **What is taken out in closed form.** As kr grows, G_zz tends to:
  - the free-space direct term between the parts of the two vias in one slab;
  - that term's images in the slab's two faces: −Gamma of it in a dielectric face (the TM Fresnel coefficient
    looking out of the slab), and the same current in a ground;
  - between neighbouring slabs, the direct term carried across the face with `2 eps_n/(eps_n + eps_m)`.

  Each of these is integrated between the two vias in closed form, statically by the antiderivative of
  `1/R`, and its retardation by Gauss's rule. The potential, at a face the via ends on, tends to
  `−/+ j w mu0 Gamma/(2 kr^3)`. It is taken out as a function with that tail and no trouble at nought,
  whose transform is `(sqrt(d^2 + rho^2) − rho)/(2 pi)`. The rest is tabulated over distance, on
  WaveGreen's path, which the two now share.
- **A run in the plane and a via.** The run sees the via's potential at its head less at its tail. By the
  formulation's reciprocity this is also what the run's own vector potential does along the via (G_zx). So
  the matrix stays symmetric, and no J1 transform or line integral of G_zx is needed.
- **Where the correction is taken.** It is taken at the cells, as the node potentials are, so that what a
  via's current adds along the runs that carry it away cancels what it adds along the via. It is taken no
  nearer a via than its cross-section's mean distance with itself, and at the ports' cells too, as though a
  port's own path up from the ground carried it. Round any path, the dielectric's part of the vias'
  inductance then cancels, as magnetostatics says it must.
- **Where a via stands.** A via to the ground stands at the centre of the cell it lands on, where its current
  enters the metal and where the runs that carry it away start. A via between metals stands where its cells
  are.
- **A via is a bar.** Its current runs on its four faces, each with the surface impedance of a quarter of its
  depth: `1/(sigma A)` at direct current, and `(1 + j)/(sigma delta)` round its perimeter once the skin is
  thinner.
- **The picture of the currents** draws a via by its current round its sides, per metre of them. That is the
  same A/m as the sheets', and the via is drawn over the metal it stands on.
- **Phase B** solves a via between metals as before: the via joins the nets it lands on, and the inductance
  takes it as a run. A via to the ground is said rather than solved: "the quasi-static extraction holds no
  conductor at the ground's potential: solve it full-wave". Every net there is free, held at a volt in turn,
  and solving a grounded pad as floating would be wrong without a word.
- `rf25_solve`'s description says a via is solved. The Via node's help says how to join a metal to the
  ground.

## What building it found

- **The correction must be taken where G_zz is.** The dielectric's part of a via's own G_zz, through 200 µm
  of 9.8, is 0.951 of its potential at its axis. It is 0.9505 of its potential at its cross-section's mean
  distance with itself (`0.2235 (a + b)` for a rectangle), the distance G_zz's own closed form uses for
  the via with itself and with its images. Taken at the axis, the two did not cancel by 5 %.
- **A port's own path is part of the cancellation.** A via's potential telescopes along the runs from the via
  to the port. Without the port's own share it ended there, short of the ground. The via's potential 1.8 mm
  away is still a tenth of its own, and the via came out 5.9 % short of vacuum through 9.8, its line's
  input impedance with a negative real part (−0.85 mΩ at 1 GHz). With the port's share, in the port's
  voltage and the same in the via's drive so that Z stays reciprocal: 2.4e-4, and passive.
- **A ground-referenced port gives no field to its own path up from the ground.** That predates vias. In a
  stack all of vacuum, where a line over its ground hardly radiates, that path's share of the radiation is
  missed. A line alone, no via, then gives back 2e-5 of a wave more than it gets (Re Z11 −9e-7 Ω at 100 MHz,
  growing as w^2), and two lines on two metals 1.6e-4. Through dielectrics the same lines are passive. It is
  the port, phase D's.
- **`runsOf` joins a via's own cells too,** as a little sheet at a height of their own, beside the vias it
  makes its own way. The full-wave solve keeps only the runs on the metals, and makes its vias itself.
- **The closed forms' series was cut off too soon** to hold the remainder to a part in 1e12 where `u s`
  nears a hundredth; a break that left the series untested found it (below).
- **The mesh's refinement at the edges cuts a strip two cells across even at "one across".** A via at a
  strip's end then lands on two cells. The tests put their vias on one cell by meshing without it, and then
  on two with an ideal short on each.
- Two slips in the tests themselves. A homogeneous check expected nothing left of the coupling of parts in
  different slabs, whose ground image the closed forms leave to the table on purpose. And the removal of an
  exploratory test duplicated the comment it sat under.

## Broken on purpose

`docs/feature_gaps/2026-11-01-rf25-vias/breaks.py` holds twenty-nine breaks:

- **The kernels:**
  - the slab's own thickness left out of the double integral;
  - G_zz over the field's permittivity;
  - the potential's TE line left out, and its sign turned;
  - the ground image the opposite current;
  - the dielectric image not taken out;
  - the direct term carried across a face as it is;
  - the potential's tail of the wrong sign;
  - the series a tenth out;
  - a via ending half way up a slab taken, and one reaching beyond the stack.
- **The solve:**
  - the potential at the via's axis;
  - the ports' share left out, of the wrong sign, and in the voltage but not the drive;
  - a run and a via not coupled, and coupled the wrong way round;
  - a via's potential at its own ends left out;
  - a via to the ground given the first cell for its tail, and stood where its cells are;
  - each via cell a run of its own;
  - the self term across the via's breadth;
  - its resistance left out;
  - its current not drawn.
- **The stack, phase B and the tab:**
  - the ground not a via's end;
  - a via to the ground from any metal;
  - the word "ground" not the ground;
  - phase B solving a grounded pad as floating;
  - the tab drawing the vias under the metals.

## What the breaks taught

- **Twenty-six of the first twenty-eight were caught at once.** Two were not, and each was a gap:
  - **The closed forms' series was never used.** It stands in for `(exp(−x) + x − 1)/x^2` where x is below
    a hundredth, and no test asked for a kr there. A test point just past the branch point does now. Its
    first run caught the series itself, cut off too soon, a part in 1e11 out where `x` neared a hundredth.
    Both series are carried further now.
  - **The check that a via spans whole slabs was redundant.** A via half way up a slab was refused all the
    same, by the check that its parts cover it, because its parts were recorded as the whole slabs. They
    are recorded as they are now, so each check has its own case, a part of a slab or a reach beyond the
    stack. The second has a test and a break of its own, the twenty-ninth.
- After those fixes the three were run again, and the kernels' eight with them: all caught, twenty-nine of
  twenty-nine.

Under the sanitizers the kernels' new tests take 1 to 16 s each, and the solve's 6 to 30 s. The full-wave
suite takes 160 s, up from 85, and the wave suite 56 s. CI's sanitized job gives a suite three times its
300 s.

## Not done

- **The mesh is not cut at the vias.** A via's current enters its cell at the cell's centre. A via to the
  ground stands there, so the correction cancels exactly. A via between metals stands where its cells are,
  and where its two cells' centres are far from it, the correction is taken a cell's breadth from where
  G_zz is: a few per cent of the dielectric's part of its inductance, which is itself a fraction of it.
- **A port's own path up from the ground** carries no field but the vias' correction. In vacuum a line over
  its ground then radiates a hair less than it should. This is phase D's ports.
- **A via to the ground in phase B,** which holds every net free. And phase B still takes a via's breadth
  as a strip's (`e^-3/2` of its side, half a square's mean distance), where the full wave takes the
  square's.
- **A via's own charge,** on its sidewall: small beside the sheets' in the thin-metal approximation.
- **De-embedding, Touchstone 2, adaptive sampling, the ground's loss, keeping the solution with the model,**
  as before.
