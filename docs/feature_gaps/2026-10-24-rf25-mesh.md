# RF 2.5D: the mesh, and what it would cost to solve

The last piece of phase A. The processed shapes of
`2026-10-24-rf25-layout-processing.md` are polygons; this divides them into cells and counts what a
solver would have to hold. No fields are computed and none are asked for: the basis functions, the
Green's functions and the matrix are phases B and C.

## What it is now

`rf25/rf25_mesh.{h,cpp}`:

- **`meshSettingsOf()`** reads the `rf_mesh` node - cells to a wavelength, the largest cell (metres,
  through the parameters), whether to refine at the edges, and the layers through the skin depth - and
  says what cannot be: fewer than four cells to a wavelength carries no current, a largest cell below
  zero is no length, and what is no number keeps its default.
- **`meshOf()`** divides each conductor. The cell size is a wavelength at the study's highest frequency,
  in the fastest dielectric of the stack, divided by the cells asked for, and never larger than the
  largest cell allowed; with neither a frequency nor a largest cell it says so rather than guessing. Each
  conductor is cut against a grid of that size with the same `clipTo()` the processing uses - exact on the
  rectangles a layout is mostly drawn with, cut to the outline elsewhere - and a conductor smaller than a
  cell is one cell.
- **The edges.** Where the current crowds, a cell that touches a conductor's boundary is divided once
  more. A cell is on a boundary when the cut took something off it or when it sits at the grid's rim; the
  inner cells are left alone, which on a 6 × 6 grid is 16 kept and 20 divided into four.
- **The cost.** The unknowns are the edges two cells of a layer share - where current flows from one cell
  to the next, which is one unknown of a rooftop basis - counted by hashing each edge's midpoint to a
  nanometre so that two cells agree whichever way round they have it. `costOf()` turns that into the
  memory a dense complex matrix would take and says it in words for the panel, and calls a mesh beyond
  what this build will set up (60,000 unknowns) too big, with what to do about it.

## Refusing before it fills swap

The proposal asks for this plainly: "The panel should show the unknown count before solving and refuse
what it cannot hold, rather than filling swap." Two refusals do it:

- A **mesh** whose shared edges exceed 60,000 is reported with its memory and marked too big, so the
  panel can show the number before anything is solved.
- A **conductor** that the cell size would cut into more cells than the whole mesh may hold is refused
  *before* it is cut, not after. One of the breaks takes that guard off, and the test then hangs rather
  than failing - a 100 µm shape at 1 nm cells is ten billion of them - so the break round runs every test
  under a timeout and counts a hang as caught. That is exactly the failure the guard prevents, and it is
  worth seeing once.

## Tested

| Test | What it holds to |
|---|---|
| `theMeshSettingsAreReadAndChecked` | The four settings read, a largest cell through a parameter, and the three things that cannot be: too few cells to a wavelength, a negative largest cell, one that is no number |
| `theCellSizeComesFromTheWavelength` | 10 GHz in eps_r 4 giving 750 µm cells and saying so, the largest cell winning where it is smaller, and neither given being refused |
| `theConductorsAreCutIntoCells` | A 4 × 2 µm conductor into eight cells whose areas sum to its own, a conductor smaller than a cell as one cell, two conductors meshed apart, and nothing to mesh said |
| `theCellsAtAnEdgeAreDividedAgain` | 36 cells plain, 16 + 20 × 4 refined, the area unchanged by the refinement, and nothing divided when it is off |
| `theUnknownsAreCountedAndTheirCostSaid` | Two cells sharing one edge, four sharing four, shapes apart sharing none, the memory said in MB and GB, the refusal beyond 60,000, and a conductor refused before it is cut |

## Broken on purpose

`docs/feature_gaps/2026-10-24-rf25-mesh/breaks.py` holds thirteen: the wavelength not deciding the size,
the largest cell not limiting it, a size guessed where nothing says one, a small conductor put on the grid
anyway, the grid not cut against the conductor, the refinement running unasked, every cell divided rather
than those at an edge, an edge cell not marked as one, every edge counted as an unknown, a mesh past the
limit not called too big, the ten-billion-cell guard removed, and the two settings checks taken out.

## What the breaks taught

Two of the first thirteen changed nothing, and each said something:

- **The special case for a conductor smaller than a cell was weight without an effect.** The grid below it
  is one square cut to the outline, which *is* that conductor, so the shortcut and the general path gave
  the same cells. It is gone, and so is its break.
- **The clip was never asked to do anything**, because every conductor in the tests was a rectangle lying
  on the grid. A break that threw the clip away and kept whole squares passed both tests. There is an
  L-shaped conductor now: of the nine squares its bounds take, five cells remain, their area is the L's
  own, and no cell's centre lies outside it. That is the case the clip exists for.

## Not done

- **The cells are squares, not triangles.** Decision 4 of the proposal is still open: rooftops on
  rectangles are what a planar layout wants and what this builds, but a shape that is not axis-aligned
  gets cells cut to its outline rather than triangles fitted to it. Phase C's basis functions will decide
  whether that is enough or whether an RWG mesh on triangles is needed beside it.
- **The refinement is one division deep.** A cell at an edge becomes four; it does not grade towards the
  boundary, and nothing measures the skin depth yet (the setting is read and reported, not used).
- **The mesh is not drawn.** The view draws the layout; drawing the cells over it, which the proposal
  asks for, is the view's own round - together with the processed shapes, which are also not drawn yet.
- **No mesh is kept with the document.** `meshOf()` is called and thrown away; a panel that shows the
  count will want it beside the model, as the multiphysics document keeps its own.
- **The unknown count is a rooftop count.** It counts shared edges, which is right for the basis the
  proposal names, and will be wrong by a constant factor for any other - the number is honest about what
  it is, and the write-up of phase C should revisit it.
